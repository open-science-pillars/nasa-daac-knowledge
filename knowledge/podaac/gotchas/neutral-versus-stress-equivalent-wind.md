# Equivalent-neutral versus real 10 m wind

**Mechanism.** A scatterometer measures the roughness of the sea
surface, and roughness is generated by the wind stress rather than by
the wind itself, so the geophysical model function that turns
backscatter into wind is trained to give the wind at 10 m that would
produce that stress under a neutral boundary layer: the equivalent
neutral (EN) wind, relative to the moving surface and without account
for the ocean current.[^measures-guide][^esdr-readme] The ESDR README
adds the detail that the calibration assumes neutral stability while
using the friction velocity from a stability-dependent
calculation.[^esdr-readme] The guide cites a 2017 paper as a review
of the issues around the supposed equivalence between the true
stress, the friction velocity and the 10 m equivalent neutral wind;
that paper's subject, the stress-equivalent reference wind, is a
quantity distinct from the equivalent neutral wind, and the sources
read here (the guide, the README and the paper's registry record,
which carries no abstract) do not state the distinction, so this
concept does not define it.[^measures-guide][^de-kloe-2017] On the OSI SAF
side, CMOD5.n is CMOD5 with a 0.7 m/s shift in input wind speed so
that the retrieved wind is a neutral wind, derived from a triple
collocation with ECMWF and buoy winds, and the 2008 calibration note
records a 0.2 m/s bias of the neutral winds against real 10 m ECMWF
winds as expected from the convention.[^calval] The OSI SAF files
name their fields `wind_speed` and `wind_dir` "at 10 m" and carry the
collocated model wind as `model_speed` and `model_dir`, with no field
naming the convention.[^podaac-b25] CCMP chose ERA5 neutral-stability
winds as its background for the same reason, that both radiometer and
scatterometer winds respond to roughness, adjusted ERA5 for the moving
surface with the OSCAR current product because satellites sense the
wind relative to the moving surface, and labels its `uwnd`, `vwnd`
and `ws` as 10 m neutral winds.[^ccmp-guide][^ccmp] The ESDR is the
one PO.DAAC product that carries both conventions side by side: the
`en_wind_*` fields and the `real_wind_*` fields, the latter computed
from the EN wind by running COARE 3.5 backwards with ERA5 2 m air
temperature, surface pressure, SST, boundary layer height and a
Magnus-approximation relative humidity, then adding the GlobCurrent
total surface current as a vector sum so that the result is no longer
surface-relative and no longer assumes neutral
stability.[^measures-guide][^esdr-readme][^podaac-esdr-b] The guide's
map of the difference shows adjustments of about 1 m/s over most of
the globe, a strong positive correction in the Southern Ocean where
eastward wind over eastward current reduces the stress relative to the
real wind, and large corrections where the sea-air temperature
difference is large, off Alaska for example, with the global average
correction likely very close to zero because of the bulk calibration
and the GMF training.[^measures-guide] The triple-collocation study
of scatterometer stress found scatterometer winds as close to real
winds as to equivalent neutral winds provided the appropriate scaling
is used, the small stability effects being masked by the uncertainty
of surface-layer models and their inputs, which is a statement about
global statistics rather than about any one region.[^portabella-2009]
Buoy comparisons in the same literature convert the anemometer wind,
measured near 3, 4 or 5 m, to a stress-equivalent 10 m wind before
comparing with ASCAT, and the anemometer height changes the
result.[^wright-2021][^measures-guide]

**Wrong-result mode.** A comparison of a scatterometer or CCMP wind
against an anemometer, a reanalysis 10 m wind or a model's real wind
that does not convert one side to the other reports the stability
correction and the surface current as a bias, and the bias has the
regional structure of the guide's difference map: it is not a constant
that averages out in a region of strong current or of large air-sea
temperature difference, however small the global mean.[^measures-guide]
The 0.2 m/s neutral-versus-real offset in the 2008 ECMWF comparison is
a global number for one year of one instrument, and quoting it as the
correction everywhere reads a mean as a field.[^calval] A wind speed
trend, a diurnal amplitude or a front's wind gradient computed from a
mixture of EN winds and real winds (a scatterometer record extended
with a reanalysis, CCMP compared against ERA5 real winds, an ESDR
`en_wind_speed` series joined to a `real_wind_speed` series) carries
the convention change as signal.[^measures-guide][^ccmp-guide] A bulk
flux computed from an EN wind with a stability-dependent transfer
coefficient applies the stability correction twice, once inside the
retrieval's definition and once in the formula, while a stress
computed from a real wind with a neutral drag coefficient applies it
not at all; the ESDR's own stress uses the EN wind with a neutral drag
coefficient precisely so that no stability adjustment is
needed.[^measures-guide] A wind relative to the fixed Earth read from
a surface-relative product is wrong by the surface current in the
current's direction, which in the Southern Ocean and the western
boundary currents is a systematic, not a random, term.[^measures-guide]
None of this raises an error: every field is a wind at 10 m in metres
per second.[^podaac-b25][^podaac-esdr-b]

**Correct approach.** A result from a scatterometer product or from
CCMP names the convention of the field it used: a 10 m equivalent
neutral wind relative to the moving surface for the ESDR `en_wind_*`
and the CCMP fields, a neutral wind by the 2008 CMOD5.n calibration
note for the OSI SAF `wind_speed` (the current streams run CMOD7.n and
the KNMI product manual was not read), or a non-neutral,
Earth-relative 10 m wind for the ESDR `real_wind_*` fields.[^measures-guide][^esdr-readme][^ccmp-guide][^podaac-esdr-b]
A comparison against an anemometer or a reanalysis converts one side
to the other's convention and states the inputs of the conversion
(the stability data, the current product, the anemometer height), or
uses the ESDR true wind, whose conversion inputs are named in the
guide and whose ancillary files carry them.[^measures-guide][^wright-2021][^dataset]
A bulk formula takes the wind kind its coefficient was derived for,
neutral drag with EN wind or stability-dependent transfer with real
wind, and says which.[^measures-guide] A series stays within one
convention, and a difference map between conventions is read as the
guide reads its own: regionally significant even when the global mean
is near zero.[^measures-guide][^portabella-2009]
